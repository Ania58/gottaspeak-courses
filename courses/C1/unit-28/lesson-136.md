# 2. Prevention or Adaptation?

Climate policy often involves two different approaches.

### Prevention

Reducing the causes of climate change.

Examples:

- reducing greenhouse gas emissions
- replacing fossil fuels with renewable energy
- improving energy efficiency
- protecting forests

### Adaptation

Preparing for consequences that can no longer be completely avoided.

Examples:

- constructing flood defences
- redesigning cities for extreme heat
- developing drought-resistant crops
- improving emergency response systems

Discuss.

1. Why do societies need both approaches?
2. Which should receive more funding today?
3. Can focusing too much on adaptation reduce motivation to prevent further climate change?
4. At what point might adaptation become impossible?

# 3. Advanced Language

Work with these terms.

- climate resilience – capacity of a community or system to withstand environmental disruption and recover effectively
- mitigation – action intended to reduce the causes or severity of climate change
- adaptive capacity – ability of a society, ecosystem or institution to adjust successfully to changing environmental conditions
- ecological tipping point – critical threshold beyond which environmental change may become difficult or impossible to reverse
- systemic vulnerability – weakness affecting an interconnected system and making it particularly susceptible to disruption
- climate displacement – forced movement of people because environmental changes make their previous living conditions unsafe or unsustainable
- environmental degradation – deterioration of the natural environment through pollution, resource depletion or ecosystem destruction
- decarbonisation – process of reducing dependence on activities and energy sources that produce carbon emissions
- climate-proof infrastructure – buildings and systems designed to remain functional under future climate conditions
- maladaptation – response to climate change that unintentionally increases vulnerability or creates additional problems

# 6. Reading

Read.

For many years, climate policy concentrated primarily on mitigation: reducing greenhouse gas emissions in an attempt to limit future warming. While mitigation remains essential, increasing attention is now being paid to adaptation because some environmental changes are already occurring and cannot be entirely prevented.

Adaptation can take many forms. Coastal cities may construct flood barriers, agricultural regions can introduce crops capable of surviving prolonged drought, and urban authorities can redesign public spaces to reduce dangerous temperatures. However, successful adaptation depends heavily on financial resources, effective institutions and long-term planning.

This creates a significant inequality. Wealthier societies are generally better equipped to protect infrastructure and respond to disasters, whereas poorer communities may contribute relatively little to global emissions while experiencing severe consequences. Furthermore, adaptation has limits. Infrastructure can be redesigned and agricultural practices can change, but certain ecosystems or settlements may eventually reach conditions to which meaningful adaptation is no longer possible.

The challenge, therefore, is not to choose between mitigation and adaptation. It is to determine how societies can pursue both without treating adaptation as an excuse to postpone difficult changes.
