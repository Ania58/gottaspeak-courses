# 3. Advanced Language

Work with these terms.

- distributive justice – principle concerned with how resources, benefits and burdens should be fairly allocated across society
- disproportionate burden – responsibility or hardship that affects one person, group or country significantly more than others
- historical responsibility – accountability based on actions taken in the past that continue to produce consequences
- economic asymmetry – substantial difference in wealth, economic power or resources between different groups or countries
- reparative measures – actions intended to compensate for or correct harm caused in the past
- structural inequality – inequality produced or maintained by economic, political or social systems rather than individual circumstances
- equitable distribution – allocation of resources according to different needs and circumstances rather than simply dividing them equally
- collective liability – shared responsibility among several parties for addressing harm or meeting an obligation
- fiscal capacity – ability of a government or institution to raise and spend financial resources
- intergenerational equity – principle that present generations should not benefit at an unfair cost to future generations

# 6. Reading

Read.

Global problems frequently expose a difficult tension between shared responsibility and unequal circumstances. Climate change provides a particularly clear example. Although its consequences affect countries around the world, nations have not contributed equally to the accumulation of greenhouse gases, nor do they possess equal resources with which to respond.

One approach is based on historical responsibility. According to this principle, countries that benefited economically from decades of intensive industrialisation should contribute more towards addressing the resulting environmental damage. Another approach emphasises current capacity: regardless of past responsibility, those with the greatest financial resources are best positioned to fund solutions.

Critics of both approaches argue that responsibility cannot remain permanently attached to historical actions and that rapidly developing economies must also accept increasing obligations. At the same time, requiring poorer countries to make comparable sacrifices may slow economic development and deepen existing inequalities.

The debate therefore involves more than deciding who caused a problem. It raises a broader question of distributive justice: how should burdens be shared when responsibility, vulnerability and the ability to pay are distributed so unevenly?

# 8. Four Principles of Fairness

Below are four possible rules for solving global problems.

### Principle A – Polluter Pays

Those who contributed most to the problem should pay the most.

### Principle B – Ability to Pay

Those with the greatest resources should contribute the most.

### Principle C – Equal Sacrifice

Every country should make a comparable economic sacrifice.

### Principle D – Shared Responsibility

Every country should contribute because the problem affects everyone.

Rank the principles from **most convincing to least convincing**.

Then test your ranking against these problems:

- climate change
- global pandemics
- refugee crises
- ocean pollution
- international food shortages

Does the same principle work equally well for every problem?

# 9. A Question of Compensation

Consider these situations.

### A

A wealthy country became prosperous partly through industries that caused significant environmental damage abroad.

Should it compensate the affected countries today?

### B

A company caused serious pollution thirty years ago. It now has completely different owners and management.

Should the company still pay?

### C

Current taxpayers are asked to compensate for decisions made by governments before they were born.

Is this fair?

### D

Future generations will experience consequences of decisions being made today.

What do present generations owe them?

Discuss the difference between **legal responsibility** and **moral responsibility** in these situations.
