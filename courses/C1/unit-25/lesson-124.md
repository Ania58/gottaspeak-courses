# 3. Language From the Topic

Work with these expressions.

- avoidance behaviour – tendency to delay or evade tasks that create discomfort or anxiety
- temporal discounting – preference for immediate rewards over larger future benefits
- executive function – set of mental processes responsible for planning, organisation and self-control
- decision paralysis – inability to act because of excessive analysis or too many options
- perfectionistic tendencies – inclination to delay action until ideal conditions or results seem possible
- self-sabotage – behaviour that unintentionally prevents personal success
- emotional regulation – ability to manage emotions in ways that support effective decision-making
- task initiation – process of starting an activity despite resistance or discomfort
- cognitive overload – reduced performance caused by excessive mental demands
- perceived competence – individual's belief in their own ability to complete a task successfully

# 6. Reading

Read.

Although procrastination is often mistaken for laziness, psychologists generally view it as a problem of emotional regulation rather than poor time management. Many people postpone tasks because beginning them creates feelings of anxiety, uncertainty or fear of failure.

Ironically, avoiding the task usually provides immediate emotional relief. However, this temporary comfort often increases stress later, creating a cycle in which procrastination becomes increasingly difficult to overcome.

Researchers have found that reducing the size of a task, eliminating distractions and focusing on simply getting started are often more effective than relying on motivation alone. In many cases, action generates motivation rather than the other way around.
