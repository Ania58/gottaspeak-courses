# 3. Language From the Topic

Work with these expressions.

- behavioural prediction – estimation of future actions based on observed patterns of behaviour
- predictive modelling – use of statistical or computational methods to forecast future outcomes
- algorithmic bias – systematic error produced when algorithms reflect incomplete or biased data
- probabilistic forecasting – prediction expressed as likelihood rather than certainty
- statistical inference – process of drawing conclusions from collected data
- behavioural analytics – study of human actions through the analysis of behavioural data
- deterministic perspective – belief that every action is caused by preceding factors and therefore potentially predictable
- human agency – capacity to make independent choices despite external influences
- predictive accuracy – degree to which a prediction corresponds to actual outcomes
- ethical implications – moral consequences arising from particular actions, decisions or technologies

# 6. Reading

Read.

Advances in artificial intelligence have significantly improved our ability to predict patterns of human behaviour. Online retailers recommend products, navigation systems anticipate traffic, and streaming platforms suggest films based on previous choices. These systems rely on enormous quantities of behavioural data rather than genuine understanding of human intentions.

Despite these achievements, prediction is rarely absolute. Human behaviour is influenced by emotions, changing circumstances, personal experiences and spontaneous decisions that cannot always be captured by historical data. Consequently, modern predictive systems usually estimate probabilities rather than certainties.

The increasing use of behavioural prediction also raises important ethical questions. While accurate forecasts may improve healthcare, education and public safety, they may also threaten privacy, reinforce existing biases and encourage decisions based on statistical assumptions rather than individual circumstances.
