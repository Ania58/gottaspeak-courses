# 3. Language From the Topic

Work with these expressions.

- conflict escalation – process through which a disagreement gradually becomes more intense or hostile
- de-escalation – deliberate actions taken to reduce tension and prevent further conflict
- emotional intelligence – ability to recognise, understand and manage emotions in oneself and others
- assertive communication – expressing opinions clearly and respectfully without becoming aggressive
- defensiveness – tendency to react by protecting oneself instead of listening openly to criticism
- mutual understanding – shared comprehension of each other's perspectives and intentions
- negotiation strategy – planned approach to reaching an acceptable agreement
- mediation – involvement of a neutral third party to help resolve a disagreement
- interpersonal conflict – disagreement arising between individuals because of differing needs, values or expectations
- constructive feedback – observations intended to improve performance without attacking the individual

# 6. Reading

Read.

Many arguments begin because people focus on defending themselves rather than understanding the other person. Once emotions become stronger than the original issue, the disagreement often shifts from solving the problem to winning the argument.

Communication researchers suggest that successful conflict resolution depends on emotional intelligence, active listening and respectful language. Instead of making accusations, effective communicators describe how a situation affects them personally and invite collaboration.

Interestingly, studies show that people rarely change their opinions during heated arguments. They are far more likely to reconsider their views after feeling respected and genuinely heard.
