# 3. Language From the Topic

Work with these expressions.

- persuasive rhetoric – language deliberately used to influence opinions or behaviour
- emotional appeal – attempt to persuade by provoking feelings rather than relying on evidence
- logical reasoning – drawing conclusions through coherent and rational argument
- credibility – quality of being trusted and believed
- cognitive framing – presenting information in a way that influences interpretation
- rhetorical device – language technique used to strengthen communication
- counterargument – argument presented in opposition to another claim
- audience perception – the way listeners interpret a speaker or message
- persuasive strategy – planned method used to influence another person's judgement
- intellectual honesty – willingness to present evidence fairly, including opposing viewpoints

# 6. Reading

Read.

Persuasion is an essential part of everyday communication. People persuade others when negotiating salaries, presenting business ideas, advertising products or simply trying to convince friends to change their minds. Effective persuasion rarely depends on a single factor. Instead, it usually combines credibility, logical reasoning and emotional engagement.

However, persuasion can easily become manipulation. While persuasion respects another person's ability to make an informed decision, manipulation often relies on deception, selective information or emotional pressure. The distinction between the two is not always obvious, which makes critical thinking an essential communication skill.

Research also suggests that people are more likely to accept information that confirms their existing beliefs. As a result, even the strongest arguments may fail if they challenge deeply held opinions.
