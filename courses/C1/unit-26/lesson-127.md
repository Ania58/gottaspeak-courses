# 3. Language From the Topic

Work with these expressions.

- implicit meaning – information suggested rather than stated directly
- explicit communication – expressing ideas clearly and directly without relying on assumptions
- communication breakdown – failure of people to understand each other effectively
- active listening – giving full attention to the speaker while seeking genuine understanding
- confirmation bias – tendency to interpret information in ways that support existing beliefs
- contextual interpretation – understanding a message according to its surrounding circumstances
- non-verbal cues – facial expressions, gestures, tone of voice and body language accompanying speech
- unintended implication – meaning that a speaker communicates accidentally
- interpersonal perception – the way people interpret each other's behaviour and intentions
- clarification strategy – technique used to confirm understanding before reacting

# 6. Reading

Read.

Misunderstandings rarely arise because people use different words. More often, they occur because people interpret the same words differently. Personal experiences, emotions, cultural background and expectations all influence how messages are understood.

Modern communication creates additional challenges. Text messages and emails often remove facial expressions, body language and tone of voice, making it easier for readers to assign meanings that the writer never intended. As a result, people frequently react to interpretations rather than to the original message.

Communication experts therefore emphasise active listening and clarification. Asking questions before drawing conclusions can prevent unnecessary conflict and improve relationships both professionally and personally.
