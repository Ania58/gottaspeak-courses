# 3. Language From the Topic

Work with these expressions.

- deliberate silence – intentional decision not to speak in order to communicate or achieve a particular effect
- non-verbal communication – transmission of meaning through facial expressions, gestures, posture and tone rather than words
- emotional restraint – ability to control emotional reactions before responding
- interpersonal awareness – sensitivity to other people's emotions, intentions and reactions
- communicative intent – purpose behind a message or behaviour
- conversational dynamics – natural flow and interaction between speakers during communication
- social signalling – communication of attitudes, intentions or social status through behaviour
- reflective pause – intentional silence used to think before responding
- attentive presence – demonstrating engagement through focused listening rather than speaking
- emotional reciprocity – mutual recognition and response to another person's emotional state

# 6. Reading

Read.

Although communication is usually associated with speaking, silence can be equally meaningful. In many cultures, pauses demonstrate respect, careful thinking or emotional sensitivity. In others, prolonged silence may be interpreted as discomfort, disagreement or uncertainty.

Communication researchers argue that effective conversations depend not only on choosing the right words but also on recognising when speaking is unnecessary. Active listening, reflective pauses and appropriate body language often encourage deeper and more honest discussions than constant verbal responses.

However, silence can also create misunderstandings when intentions are unclear. Consequently, successful communicators learn to balance verbal expression with attentive listening and thoughtful pauses.
