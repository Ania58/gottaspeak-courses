# 3. Language From the Topic

Work with these expressions.

- obedience to authority – tendency to follow instructions given by individuals perceived as having legitimate power
- social conformity – adjustment of behaviour or opinions to match those of a group
- moral courage – willingness to act ethically despite personal risk or social pressure
- diffusion of responsibility – phenomenon in which individuals feel less personally responsible when others are present
- bystander effect – reduced likelihood of helping because other people are also witnessing the situation
- institutional pressure – influence exerted by organisations that encourages conformity or obedience
- moral disengagement – psychological process through which people justify behaviour that conflicts with their ethical standards
- ethical accountability – personal responsibility for the moral consequences of one's actions
- peer pressure – influence exerted by people of similar status encouraging particular behaviours or attitudes
- independent judgement – ability to evaluate situations according to one's own ethical reasoning rather than external influence

# 6. Reading

Read.

History provides numerous examples of ordinary individuals participating in unethical actions while believing they were simply following instructions or fulfilling their duties. Psychological research suggests that social conformity, obedience to authority and institutional pressure can significantly influence behaviour, even among people who generally consider themselves moral.

However, these influences do not completely remove personal responsibility. Although external pressure may explain why people behave in certain ways, many ethicists argue that individuals remain accountable for their choices, particularly when those choices harm others.

For this reason, organisations increasingly encourage employees to question unethical practices, report misconduct and develop independent ethical judgement rather than relying solely on authority.
