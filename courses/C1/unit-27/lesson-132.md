# 3. Language From the Topic

Work with these expressions.

- rule of law – principle that everyone, including governments, is subject to the law
- civil liberties – fundamental rights and freedoms protected by law
- legal framework – system of laws regulating society
- government intervention – action taken by public authorities to influence individual or social behaviour
- proportionality – legal principle that restrictions should not exceed what is necessary to achieve a legitimate objective
- constitutional rights – freedoms guaranteed by a country's constitution
- legal precedent – previous court decision used to guide future cases
- public interest – welfare or benefit of society as a whole
- ethical legislation – laws designed to reflect moral principles as well as legal standards
- individual autonomy – right to make personal decisions without unnecessary external interference

# 6. Reading

Read.

Although law and morality often overlap, they are not identical. Many actions that are considered immoral are perfectly legal, while some laws have historically been regarded as unjust. Throughout history, legal systems have evolved as societies reconsidered their moral values and expectations.

Modern democracies attempt to balance individual freedom with the protection of society. Governments therefore face difficult decisions when determining whether behaviour should be regulated by law or left to personal responsibility. Excessive regulation may restrict civil liberties, whereas insufficient regulation may expose vulnerable people to harm.

As a result, legal systems constantly adapt to new technologies, cultural changes and ethical challenges. Debates surrounding artificial intelligence, privacy and freedom of expression demonstrate that deciding what should be legal is rarely straightforward.
