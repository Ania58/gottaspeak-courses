# 3. Language From the Topic

Work with these expressions.

- moral dilemma – situation in which every available choice involves an ethical disadvantage
- ethical principle – fundamental moral rule used to guide behaviour and decision-making
- moral responsibility – obligation to accept accountability for the consequences of one's actions
- conflicting values – ethical beliefs that cannot all be satisfied simultaneously
- unintended consequences – outcomes that occur despite not being planned or intended
- ethical justification – reasoned explanation demonstrating why a decision is morally acceptable
- moral integrity – consistent commitment to one's ethical values even under pressure
- accountability – willingness to accept responsibility for decisions and their consequences
- ethical judgement – evaluation of actions according to moral standards
- moral obligation – duty to act according to ethical principles regardless of personal benefit

# 6. Reading

Read.

Ethical dilemmas arise when two or more moral principles conflict and no solution is completely satisfactory. Unlike ordinary problems, ethical dilemmas rarely have objectively correct answers because different people prioritise different values, such as honesty, loyalty, fairness or compassion.

Philosophers often distinguish between judging an action by its intentions and judging it by its consequences. Some argue that a morally good intention is the most important factor, while others believe that the outcome ultimately determines whether a decision was right or wrong.

In everyday life, ethical decisions are often influenced not only by personal values but also by social expectations, emotions and cultural norms. Consequently, people facing the same dilemma may reach very different conclusions while sincerely believing they have acted ethically.
