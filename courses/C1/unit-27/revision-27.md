# 4. Language From the Listening

Work with these expressions.

- moral certainty – strong conviction that one's moral judgement is unquestionably correct
- moral absolutism – belief that certain moral principles apply universally regardless of context
- projectivism – philosophical idea that people project subjective experiences onto the world and experience them as objective qualities
- moral pluralism – view that more than one moral principle or way of living may be valid
- moral evolution – gradual adaptation of ethical norms and practices as societies and circumstances change
- moral inflexibility – inability or unwillingness to reconsider ethical beliefs when confronted with new circumstances
- normative framework – structured set of principles used to determine how people ought to behave
- moral relativism – view that moral judgement may depend on cultural, historical or social context
- inherited moral assumptions – ethical beliefs absorbed from family, culture or community without deliberate examination
- ethical adaptability – capacity to revise moral rules and principles in response to new challenges while retaining ethical responsibility
